The line between product and marketing has been dissolving for years. But most product managers still treat growth as someone else's problem — a separate team, a separate set of metrics, a separate language entirely.

That's a mistake.

## The PM Who Doesn't Understand Acquisition Is Flying Blind

Here's the thing about building great products: **it doesn't matter how good the experience is if nobody finds it.** And the way people find your product — the channels, the messaging, the friction points — those are product decisions whether you call them that or not.

When a PM understands growth marketing, they can:

- Design onboarding flows that align with the promises made in ad copy
- Prioritize features that actually reduce CAC, not just increase engagement
- Speak the same language as the marketing team during planning

> The best product decisions I've ever made came from understanding how users arrived, not just what they did after they got there.

## Three Skills Every PM Should Steal From Growth

### 1. Funnel Thinking

Growth marketers live and die by funnels. Every stage has a conversion rate, every rate is a lever. PMs should adopt this same ruthless clarity when evaluating feature impact.

Instead of asking *"will users like this feature?"*, ask *"which stage of the funnel does this improve, and by how much?"*

### 2. Experiment Velocity

The growth marketing playbook is built on rapid experimentation. Launch fast, measure everything, kill what doesn't work. PMs can apply this to:

1. Feature launches (ship smaller, learn faster)
2. Pricing experiments (test before you commit)
3. Positioning tests (change the frame, not the product)

### 3. Attribution Awareness

Understanding where value comes from is a superpower. When you can trace a user from first touch to activation to revenue, you can make informed tradeoffs about what to build next.

```
First Touch → Activation → Revenue
     ↓              ↓          ↓
  Channel ROI   Feature Value  LTV Impact
```

## The Payoff

PMs who understand growth don't just build better products — they build products that **grow themselves**. They make decisions that compound because they understand the full lifecycle, not just the in-app experience.

If you're a PM who has never sat in on a media buying call, never audited a landing page, never looked at a channel attribution report — start there. The perspective shift is worth more than any framework or certification.

*The best PMs aren't just product thinkers. They're growth thinkers who happen to own the product.*
